### Claim 4 — Torque verification

- ICC-ES AC358 (public copy of the June 2012 revision, helicalpileworld.com; the current AC358 (24) is paywalled at https://icc-es.org/acceptance-criteria/ac358/): Section 3.13.2 "Evaluation reports shall include a correlation between final installation torque, T," and default values "2.875-inch outside diameter round shafts Kt = 9 ft-1", "3.0-inch ... Kt = 8 ft-1", "3.5-inch ... Kt = 7 ft-1", "1.5-inch- and 1.75-inch-square shafts Kt = 10 ft-1".
- Hubbell/CHANCE engineering article: "Qult = Kt x T", citing IBC Section 1810.3.3.1.9 and Hoyt and Clemence (1989); Perko (2009) regression "Kt = 22.285(d eff)^-0.9195" added to AC358.
- CCMC 13675-R (NRC): "there is a direct relationship between the applied torque and the allowable compressive and tensile loads"; where granular material exceeds 200 mm, "the allowable compressive and tensile loads have to be confirmed by on-site load tests."
- CCMC 13608-R: "A series of 30 tests was performed" at four sites "to determine a correlation between the torque applied during installation and the allowable loads."
- Techno Metal Post FAQ: "The maximum load capacity is determined based on the torque measured during the installation".
- GoliathTech FAQ on how torque is measured: a PSI gauge on the hydraulic motor converted to torque, or a direct torque-measuring device.

Wording rule: "torque-verified" is supportable; "calibrated" requires the owner's calibration certificate for the gauge/indicator (date, serial) and must name the CCMC report whose torque table is used.

### Claim 5 — Frost protection guarantee and "zero risk"

- Hubbell/CHANCE technical article: defines adfreeze ("ice can stick (bond) to nearby surfaces—like concrete, steel, or wood"); one-winter comparison "the drilled pier showed a heave of about 1.4 in. (36 mm) while the helical pile only heaved about 0.2 in. (5 mm)"; "The adfreeze stress is higher on drilled concrete piers because of the surface roughness". Same article: frost jacking is countered only if resistance below the frost zone exceeds adfreeze uplift, i.e., it is a design check, not an absolute.
- CCMC 13059-R (NRC, Techno Metal Post): the shaft "is covered with a ribbed polyethylene pipe, which acts as a frost sleeve to isolate the pile from being jacked up by annual frost heave".
- CCMC 13675-R and 13691-R: piles "must be screwed into the ground to below the frost line using mechanized equipment."
- PileConnect (engineer-authored article, Canadian practice): the Canadian Foundation Engineering Manual adfreeze design values are "100 kPa for steel in fine-grained soils and 150 kPa for steel in saturated gravel", tracing to Penner's 1974 Ottawa field study. Treat as secondary; the CFEM itself was not retrieved.
- CGS GeoEdmonton 2018 paper (members.cgs.ca) addresses adfreeze piles in permafrost; not a seasonal-frost reference, so not used for wording.
- No CMHC document on frost jacking was found (searches returned engineering and solar-industry sources only).

Wording rule: "Guaranteed" only with the owner's written warranty (term, exclusions, URL). "Zero frost heave risk" is CUT. Publishable mechanism copy: "Frost grips a rough concrete pier far harder than a smooth galvanized steel shaft. Our piles are driven below the local frost depth and anchored by the helix, so seasonal frost has little to lift and little to lift with. Frost movement is designed against, not eliminated."

### Claim 6 — Year-round installation

- Techno Metal Post FAQ: "We can install piles year-round, including winter. If necessary, we can pre-drill the area with an auger or use our 'Thermarod' to thaw the ground".
- Techno Metal Post, Installing Helical Piles in Winter (dealer interview): clear snow, "uses a type of auger to pre-drill a hole and break the ice and/or the frozen ground"; thawing "takes about half an hour to thaw a 12-inch radius"; "the best time to install piles being from April to the end of December"; winter installs "more common for large commercial projects".
- Postech home page: "Clay, rock, sand, or frozen ground, our installers work year-round"; Postech Moncton blog (https://pileinstaller.ca/blog/installing-helical-pile-foundation-in-winter): "we can absolutely install helical piles in winter. With the right tools".
- GoliathTech home and Halton installer pages: "Year-round installation". Ontario installer Diamondtech: "mechanically driven deep below the frost line into stable, non-frozen soil" in winter.

Caveats to carry into copy: pre-drilling or thawing is required through the frost crust; extra time; possible surcharge; manufacturers describe winter work as less common for residential.

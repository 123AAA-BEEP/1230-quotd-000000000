## Notes and evidence by claim

### Claim a — What ICF is, how the wall is built, and CCMC status

What the sources actually say:

- Nudura 10-part specification (Tremco Canada Division, RPM Canada, 220 Wicksteed Avenue, Toronto; PDF on nudura.com): forms "consist of two uniform thickness panels of expanded polystyrene (EPS) foam plastic insulation" joined by "polypropylene plastic insert webs" and "hinged web/fastening strips"; the web/fastening strips "run full height of the form panels and are embedded within EPS at regular 8 inch (203 mm) intervals", "recessed ½" (12.7 mm) from the EPS surface" with a "fastening flange that measures 1 ½" (38 mm) in width" that provides "anchorage surface for attachment of interior and exterior finishes". Standard form 96 in long, 18 in high; widths 9 ¼ to 17 ¼ in "to enable formation of 4-inch, 6-inch, 8-inch, 10-inch and 12-inch (102 mm, 152 mm, 203 mm, 254 mm and 305 mm) thick flat monolithic concrete walls".
- Nudura pre-design criteria page: "2 5/8-inch (67 mm) thick EPS (Expanded Polystyrene) insulation panels on each side of the concrete core wall"; core choice may be driven by "high clay content in soils, seismic activity, or high wind pressures".
- Logix material property data sheet (updated 12/10/19, mirrored at bvrthermal.com): EPS "R-Value (Thermal Resistance) per inch (per 25.4mm) R 4.13 (RSI 0.72)" by ASTM C518; density "27.5kg/m3 (1.72pcf)"; polypropylene web shear strength "26.1MPa"; referenced standards include "CAN/ULC S717, Standard for Flat Wall Insulating Concrete Form (ICF) Units - Material Properties", ASTM E2634 and CAN/ULC S701; a listing number "QM0503" appears beside the standards list.
- 2024 Compendium Vol 1, Article 9.15.4.1: "Flat wall insulating concrete form units shall conform to CAN/ULC-S717.1". Sentence 9.3.1.1.(4): for flat ICF walls ≤2 storeys and ≤3 m floor-to-floor in light-frame buildings, concrete per CSA A23.1 "with a maximum aggregate size of 19 mm", reinforcing to CSA G30.18, "minimum specified yield strength of 400 MPa", lapped "450 mm for 10M bars and 650 mm for 15M bars".
- 9.15.4.2.(2): the core "shall be not less than the greater of (a) 150 mm, or (b) the thickness of the concrete in the wall above". 9.15.4.5.(1): horizontal steel "one 10M bar placed not more than 300 mm from the top of the wall" and "10M bars spaced not more than 600 mm o.c." in the inside half with 30 mm cover; (2) vertical steel per Tables 9.15.4.5.-A/-B/-C for 150, 190 and 240 mm walls. 9.20.17.1: above-ground core "not less than 140 mm" and constant; 9.20.17.2: vertical "10M bars spaced not more than 400 mm o.c." in the middle third.

### Claim b — Ontario Building Code position and municipal permit practice

Code text retrieved from the 2024 Compendium (digital copy; confirm against e-Laws before quoting verbatim):

- Scope, foundations, 9.15.1.1.(1)(c): Section 9.15 applies to "flat insulating concrete form foundation walls and concrete footings not subject to surcharge" on "stable soils with an allowable bearing pressure of 75 kPa or greater" in "buildings of light-frame or flat insulating concrete form construction that are not more than 2 storeys in building height, with a maximum floor-to-floor height of 3 m". Sentence (2): other foundations "shall be designed in accordance with Section 9.4" (i.e., Part 4 structural design).
- Scope, above ground, 9.20.1.1.(1)(b): flat ICF walls not in contact with the ground that "have a maximum floor-to-floor height of 3 m", "not more than 2 storeys", and where "Sa(0.2), is not greater than 0.4". Otherwise designed for specified loads (Part 4). 9.3.1.1.(3): except for 9.15.4 and 9.40, "reinforced concrete shall be designed to conform to the requirements of Part 4".
- Table 9.15.4.2.-A (solid concrete and concrete core in flat wall ICF): for a 150 mm core at 15 MPa, maximum height of finished ground above basement floor is 0.8 m unsupported at top and 1.5 m supported at top (wall ≤2.5 m); a 200 mm core allows 1.2 m / 2.15 m; at 20 MPa the 150 mm core allows 0.8 m / 1.8 m and 200 mm allows 1.2 m / 2.3 m. Walls over 3.0 m unsupported height are outside the table.
- 9.15.4.2.(2.1): ICF foundation walls "shall be laterally supported at the top and at the bottom". 9.15.4.4: laterally supported at the bottom where the wall "supports backfill not more than 1.2 m", or has "a shear key" plus floor framing, or is dowelled "15M bars spaced not more than 1.2 m o.c., or 10M bars spaced not more than 600 mm o.c." 9.15.4.3.(5): supported at the top if joists are installed per 9.20.17.5 (ledger with anchor bolts embedded ≥100 mm, Table 9.20.17.5 spacing).
- 9.20.17.3–.4: opening rules (none within 1,200 mm of corners; lintels over openings wider than 900 mm in loadbearing walls, Span Tables 9.20.17.4.-A/B/C; stirrups over 1,200 mm). 9.20.17.7: above-ground ICF walls "shall be protected from precipitation and damage in conformance with Section 9.27."

Municipal practice (what the six sites publish):

- Toronto, When to Call for Inspection of Small Buildings (Part 9): "Footings at completion of formwork, before pouring concrete", "Foundations* at completion", then framing, insulation and vapour barrier, etc. The Homeowner's Guide to Building Permits (PDF, 2017) words the second stage "at completion of footings and foundations and prior to backfill" (https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/9273-Building-Permit-Guide-WEB.pdf). - Hamilton, Building Inspections: "Footing Inspection: Prior to pouring footings: Formwork and reinforcing for footings, column pads, and structural slabs-on-grade." "Backfill: Prior to backfilling foundation excavations: Drainage layer, weeping tile, and stone cover for the weeping tile." Building Division 905-546-2720 (from the helical register).
- Mississauga, Book a building permit inspection: "Book your footing inspection before placing any concrete"; inspector checks soil "such as 75 kilopascals (kPa) for typical residential design", that "All required reinforcement ... is installed according to the approved drawings" and formwork matches "footing width, thickness and location". Drawings page: "strongly recommended that you work with a design professional such as an architect, professional engineer or qualified designer".
- Oakville, Building Inspection Scheduling Process: "Excavation/footing forms (prior to pouring concrete)" and "Pre-backfill (Site survey including top of foundation elevation to be submitted prior to inspection for new construction)". - Burlington, Building Permits page: for some projects "the drawings must be stamped by an architect, professional engineer or both. Refer to Division C, Section 1.2"; the Booking an Inspection page loaded but its stage list was not extractable as text.
- Guelph: only the Building Permits and Inspections landing page resolved (https://guelph.ca/living/house-and-home/building-permits/); inspection-stage URLs returned 404. Record as not retrieved.
- ICF-specific text: none of the six sites mentions ICF. OAA Practice Tip PT.36.3 (v3.0, 2025-09-19) notes that for ICF "some AHJs may request documentation to confirm compliance. This may include product literature, third-party thermal resistance testing". Contractor sites (icfpro.ca) assert "Stamped engineering drawings required for most ICF builds in Ontario"; treat as a contractor's practice statement, not a municipal rule.

Why b4 is REWORDED: the Code provides a prescriptive Part 9 path for ICF within stated limits, so "engineered drawings are always required" is not supportable; "the CCMC report is required" is wrong for S717.1-conforming flat-wall ICF (see claim a). Publish the conditional wording in the table.

### Claim c — Energy: SB-12 and R-values

- 2024 MMA Supplementary Standard SB-12 (Compendium Vol 2), Article 3.1.1.2 "Energy Efficiency for Buildings Located in Zone 1": (1) envelope "shall conform to Table 3.1.1.2.A" (AFUE ≥92%); (2) Table 3.1.1.2.B for 84–92% AFUE; (3) Table 3.1.1.2.C for electric heat. - Table 3.1.1.2.A (SI), packages A1–A6. Walls Above Grade, min nominal RSI: A1 3.87; A2 3.34 + 0.88 ci; A3 2.46 + 1.32 ci; A4 3.87 + 0.88 ci; A5 3.34 + 0.88 ci; A6 3.87 + 0.88 ci; min effective RSI 3.00 / 3.58 / 3.28 / 3.77 / 3.58 / 3.77. Note (1): nominal values are "for the thermal insulation component only"; note (4): effective values "include entire basement wall or slab assembly components and interior air film". Note (6) allows RSI 3.52 ci and RSI 2.11 + 1.76 ci to be interchanged. Conversions used on the site: RSI × 5.678 = R; SB-12 itself writes "RSI 1.76 (R10)" and "RSI 3.52 ci (R20)".
- ICF rule, Sentences 3.1.1.2.(5)–(6): an ICF wall "that has minimum RSI 1.76 (R10) insulation on the interior surface of the concrete and minimum RSI 1.76 (R10) insulation on the exterior surface ... is deemed to comply with the thermal values set out for walls in the compliance packages in Table 3.1.1.2.A" and "is permitted to be used in lieu of basement walls that require insulation value of RSI 3.52 ci (R20) or less." - Manufacturer nominal values seen: Amvic page title "Buy R22 & R30 Insulated Concrete Forms"; Fox Blocks "An R-value of 23.5+"; Quad-Lock "two continuous layers of insulation rated at R-22 and higher"; Logix R 4.13 per inch (assembly value referred to "Logix Design Manual, Section 8.5", not retrieved); Nudura 2 5/8 in EPS each side (no assembly R printed on the pages retrieved).
- Nominal vs effective vs thermal mass: Quad-Lock's energy page argues studs "R-5 or R-7" bridge frame walls while "thermal bridging is absent" in ICF, and describes "Thermal Mass ... capacity to store heat" as a separate effect. These are manufacturer arguments; no NRC or CMHC measurement was retrieved. Fox Blocks lists an RDH Laboratories "Energy Code Review Canada" document, not fetched.
- Savings percentages: icfpro.ca claims "25-40% heating energy savings" citing "RDH Labs measured" with no link; carbondaleconstruction.ca and BuildBlock make unsourced savings statements. CUT until a primary report is supplied.

### Claim d — Cold-weather concreting

- Concrete Ontario (Ready Mixed Concrete Association of Ontario) "Cold Weather Concreting" technical specification (PDF, uploaded 2024-12), reproducing CSA A23.1:24/A23.2:24 with CSA permission: "cold weather is when there is a probability of the air temperature falling below 5 °C within 24 h of placing". Table 14, permissible concrete temperature at placing: sections under 0.3 m thick minimum 10 °C, maximum 32 °C; 0.3 to under 1 m: 10 / 30 °C; 1 to under 2 m: 5 / 25 °C; 2 m and over: 5 / 20 °C. Curing: "maintain the concrete temperature at a minimum of 10 °C for the duration of the curing period"; Table 19 basic curing "3 d at ≥ 10 °C or for the time necessary to attain 40% of the specified strength", additional curing "7 d total at ≥ 10 °C and ... 70%"; "Concrete must achieve a minimum strength of 7 MPa to withstand early frost damage". Preparation: "Remove all snow and ice from surfaces before placing concrete", "Avoid using calcium chloride or de-icing salts as de-icing agents in forms", "Do not place concrete on surfaces that would lower its temperature below the limits in Table 14"; protection "by means of heated enclosures, coverings, insulation, or a suitable combination". Heating of mix water or aggregate: not stated in this sheet; the CSA clause itself is paywalled: null.
- Ontario P.Eng blog (parsways.ca, Feb 2023, secondary): below 5 °C "hydration slows dramatically"; winter concrete "must meet OBC and CSA A23.1 standards".
- Manufacturer statements: Nudura installation manual s.6.0: EPS forms enhance curing by "Giving excellent thermal protection in the cold weather and extreme heat" and "Controlling moisture loss inside the concrete while curing"; pre-pour checklist: "If in winter construction, has form cavity been protected against snow or ice build-up"; post-pour: "Cold weather pouring – has top of wall been protected from freezing?". Concrete Alberta (association): "the technology exists to properly cure cement in any kind of weather so you can avoid cold weather delays or added costs for hoarding." icfpro.ca (contractor): winter pours carry a "20-30% premium on concrete during shoulder seasons".

Wording rule: "year-round" is supportable only with the CSA caveats (placing temperature, 10 °C protection period, 7 MPa before frost, top-of-wall and footing protection, heated mix from the supplier) and a note that winter work adds cost.

## A1. Provider layer is internal-only (amends §5.1, §5.4, Loop 2)

**Decision:** consumer pages contain **no provider talk of any kind** — no names, no counts, no anonymized aggregates, no "we find the specialists" meta-copy, no roster claims. Consumers on a quotes site assume supply exists; explaining it reads as defensive. The page sells the service plainly: describe the project → get a quote.

- The provider pipeline (Places + permits + licenses + manual curation) still runs at full strength. Its output is an **internal sales asset**: it is how leads get sold. Not rendering it publicly also means competitors cannot scrape our curated supply.
- Scarcity may appear only as *advice* where true and sourced ("limewash is specialist work most painters don't offer") — education about the trade, never claims about our roster.
- `/pro/` provider profile pages: **not built at launch.** The claim-your-profile loop (spec Loop 2) is deferred until the owner green-lights the two-sided phase.
- **Publish gate 2 amended:** from "≥1 named provider on page" to "≥1 sellable provider identified in the database, or a documented scarce-market exception approved by the owner." Same rigor — a page whose leads have no buyer still doesn't publish — but enforcement is fully internal.

## A2. Claim classes — the anti-fake copy rule (extends §5.3, §8.1)

Every consumer-visible claim must belong to one of three classes:

1. **Policy claims** (things we control): "free, no obligation". Allowed always — they are commitments, not statistics.
2. **Public-data claims** (sourced, verifiable): permit counts, licensing counts, climate normals, housing-stock shares. Allowed with source attribution. Scarcity is expressed through real public data plus honest vagueness ("a handful"), never a manufactured precise count.
3. **Measured-performance claims** (response times, match rates, "based on N quotes"): **locked until actually measured**, then rendered only with provenance ("based on 214 requests since March 2026").

Precise numbers without provenance are the fake-social-proof pattern §8.1 bans. This rule is CI-lintable (numeric claims must carry a source annotation in the template data).

## A3. Lead system at launch: no SMS OTP (amends §3.1, §6.1, §6.3)

**Decision:** launch without the Twilio dependency — it was the longest external lead time in the plan (A2P 10DLC registration: days to weeks).

- Launch flow: two-step form (project details → contact + consent) → server-side validation → write to Supabase `leads` → email notification to **leads@getquotd.com**.
- Anti-spam at launch: honeypot field + time-to-complete check + phone/email format validation. No CAPTCHA.
- SMS OTP is a **post-launch enhancement**, added if/when lead volume or buyer feedback justifies it. The edge function keeps the OTP feature flag so it can be enabled without rework.
- **Owner override recorded (2026-08-01):** §6.3's "phone-verified only" anti-Bark commitment is amended for launch to "validated-contact leads, honestly represented." Lead buyers are told plainly what verification level a lead carries. Exclusive-or-max-3 and dead-lead credit commitments are unchanged.

## A4. Hero imagery rules (extends §8.2)

- **Aesthetic niches** (limewash, venetian plaster, herringbone…): one real, rights-cleared material/finish photograph **per niche** (not per city), macro/editorial, consistent art direction, captioned like a field guide. First source: owner's own limewash project photos from the torontolimewash.com venture; later, provider portfolio photos with rights secured at claim time.
- **Functional niches** (french drains, radon, egress windows…): a clean sectional line diagram of how the system works — more persuasive than photography and unmistakably ours.
- Layout: heroes stay typography-led (H1 → answer block → data band → form rail). The image is a captioned band within/below the hero, never a full-bleed banner pushing content below the fold, never the LCP bottleneck.
- Fallback: niches without a real photo ship the pure typographic hero. No stock, no AI imagery, no skyline clip-art, ever (§8.1 stands).

## A5. Head terms use the consumer's person-form (extends §9, informed by measured data)

Evidence: owner's torontolimewash.com micro-site GSC (3-mo): "limewash **painter** toronto" 322 impressions @ pos 3.5; "limewash **painter**" 257 @ 5.6; person-form variants dominate. Also "limewash **paint** toronto" 347 impressions @ 13.7 = product intent (see A6).

- `consumer_terms` on each niche is **required** and drives titles + H1s (e.g. H1 "Limewash painters in Toronto", not "Limewash painting services").
- Title pattern: `{Consumer term} in {City}: Cost, Season & Who Does It ({Year})` — year only where data actually refreshes.
- The micro-site's full GSC query export is P0 calibration data (the only *measured* demand numbers we own). Micro-site 301 into `getquotd.com` at launch: decision deferred to P2 (owner's call; recommendation on file is to 301).

## A6. Editorial/guides layer expanded (extends §3.2 `/guides/`)

Per-niche editorial clusters, not an unstructured blog:

- Content types: supplier/product rankings (e.g. limewash paint brands — Romabio class), cost deep-dives, failure-mode stories, hiring guides, seasonal timing pieces.
- Rationale (measured): product-intent queries out-volume hire-intent ones ("limewash paint toronto", 347 impressions — the largest single query on the owner's test site). Guides catch buyers one search earlier and feed authority to money pages via internal links.
- Rules: rankings carry stated, defensible criteria (FTC + Google reviews-quality posture); spec §2 trademark discipline ("works with" phrasing, non-affiliation footer disclaimer); no affiliate links at launch; claim-classes rule (A2) applies.
- Launch scope: ~5 opening guides (per P2), weighted toward the niches with proven product-intent volume.

## A7. Launch geometry confirmed (affirms §3.3 against the "one city × all niches" alternative)

Considered and rejected: Toronto × all services. Rare-niche demand per metro is thin (measured: single-digit monthly clicks at position ~3.5 for the founding niche in Canada's largest metro); SEO authority accrues per *topic*, not per city; a single-climate test can't demonstrate the data layer's differentiator.

- **10 niches × 30–50 cities stands.** Concentration happens on the *operations* axis instead: city lists deliberately overlap on a shared core (GTA + top NA metros), Toronto is the P1 proof-page metro and first lead-sales territory.

## A9. CTA is singular and service-like (amends §5.1 form copy, extends A1)

**Decision:** the call to action is **"Get a quote"** (brand verb: "Get quoted") — obvious, singular, zero mechanics. No "up to 3 quotes," no "we shop your request around," no marketplace framing anywhere in consumer-facing marketing copy. The site should feel like *the service*, not a platform that brokers quotes. Consequences:

- The max-3 routing rule (§6.3) remains fully in force as **operational policy** — it governs how leads are sold, and it appears in the consent fine print where the law requires disclosing who may contact the user. It is simply not a marketing message.
- Form microcopy shrinks to policy claims that serve the consumer directly: "Free, no obligation."

## A10. Instant-estimate tool (future feature — deferred, not in launch scope)

Owner concept, assessed and parked for P3+: address-in → ballpark-quote-out for *measurable exterior niches* (gutters, holiday lighting, exterior limewash, pressure washing, driveway sealing). Feasible without computer vision for v1: geocode → building footprint polygon from open data (Microsoft Building Footprints / Overture Maps) → perimeter & area math → 2–3 questionnaire variables (stories, detached/semi/row, material) → range from the same wage-localized cost model that powers the cost tables. Show the measured inputs ("~142 linear ft of roofline") and let the user correct them; wide honest bands; claim-classes rule applies. v2 options: Google Solar API roof segments (eave-vs-gable for gutter accuracy, per-request cost), street-view vision parsing (last resort — a questionnaire tap beats CV on cost and reliability).

Strategic value: mechanically differentiated lead magnet (survival-rule-native — real computation, not prose); leads arrive with structured measurements, which raises their sale value; "cost calculator" query class is a real SEO surface. Risks: anchor-price blowback from providers (mitigate with wide ranges + "specialist confirms on site"), party-wall geometry on attached homes, per-niche fit (useless for diagnostic niches — radon, french drains). Build trigger: after launch batch is indexed and first leads are selling; best-timed niche = holiday lighting pre-season (Sept–Oct).

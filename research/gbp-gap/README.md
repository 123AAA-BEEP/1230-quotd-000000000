## 1. The thesis, stated precisely

**What a GBP category does.** Every Google Business Profile carries one primary category and up to nine secondary ones, chosen from Google's fixed list of 4,044 (US and Canada lists are identical for home services). The primary category is the strongest single ranking input for the local pack. When someone searches a service term, Google builds the pack from profiles whose categories match the query's intent; profile "services" text, reviews and website content add weaker relevance.

**What happens when the service is not a category.** For "limewash painters toronto" there is no category to match, so Google falls back to the nearest one ("Painter"). Four things follow:

1. **The pack is absent or wrong.** Either no map block appears, or it shows painters who do not do the work. Either way the pack absorbs fewer clicks and users scroll to organic.
2. **No Local Services Ads block.** Canada has only 17 LSA home-service categories (the US has 41): no painter, landscaper, foundation, general contractor, pool, insulation or drain category. For 22 of the 32 representative terms checked, no LSA unit can appear in Toronto.
3. **The directory wall is thinner.** HomeStars, Houzz and Yelp rank with category pages. Fourteen of the 32 terms have no Canadian LSA category and no exact Houzz or Yelp category: limewash, venetian plaster, underpinning, tuckpointing, basement apartment legalization, asbestos removal, vermiculite, radon, interlock repair, armour stone, pool removal, soundproofing, stair lift, heated driveway.
4. **Contractors have not built pages.** Businesses optimise around the category they can pick, so few have a page that names the niche. The first page that names the service exactly, in the consumer's own words, becomes the clearest entity match.

**What the gap does not do.**

- It does not create demand. Volume is set by consumer vocabulary. Rubric criterion 2 (searchability) still governs.
- It is bounded by the free-text "services" field. Google says a custom service "may be highlighted" when someone searches for it, and that it "can also detect category information from your website". A painter who lists "limewash painting" still appears for the query, with a weaker match. The gap is a relevance handicap for incumbents, not a guaranteed empty pack.
- It gives nothing where the term is a category and the results are platform-locked (roofing, plumbing, kitchen remodelling). Those stay excluded; only their craft or regulatory slices enter under addendum A11 Track 2.
- It does not change the survival rule. Indexation is still decided by real local data on the page.

**How the lens fits the spec.** It is a sharper instrument for rubric criterion 4 (platform gap) and a good predictor of the SERP beatability scored in P0. Recommendation: add two fields to every niche record, `gbp_coverage` (EXACT, PARTIAL or NONE) and `gbp_nearest` (the verbatim category Google falls back to). NONE and PARTIAL are eligible on either track. EXACT is eligible only as a Track 2 craft slice. A data-field addition, within builder discretion.

## 2. Method

- **Ticket tiers** (typical installed job in the GTA, CAD): T1 ≥ C$30k; T2 C$10k–30k; T3 C$2.5k–10k. Below C$2.5k is deferred to pass 2 (§7).
- **Three levels.** SERVICE (what you hire, 138 rows), USE (what it is applied to or the project type, 26) and OUTCOME (the problem the homeowner types, 47). Uses and outcomes are the connective tissue of the web (§5); most live as sections, FAQ or guides rather than as their own URLs.
- **Ground truth first.** One agent joined two independent mirrors of the GBP list by Google's category ID (4,044 rows each, identical), cross-checked a third, and then classified every candidate slug. Domain agents recorded a belief; the ground-truth pass overrides it (66 of 211 beliefs changed, mostly NONE → PARTIAL under the convention that "Remodeler", "Home builder" and "Carpenter" are specific trades, and that an outcome takes the class of the service that solves it).
- **GTA lens.** Six domain agents each covered one cluster with GTA prices from Canadian sources (HomeStars price guides, RenoAssistance, contractor price pages, City of Toronto, Ontario programs), GTA drivers (housing stock, climate, bylaws, subsidies, insurance) and the data hooks a page could use. Every price carries a URL; estimates are marked EST; † marks a figure seen only in a search snippet.
- **Primary data pulled directly:** Toronto Open Data building permits (203,904 active rows, keyword counts and declared values are our own analysis), Toronto Heritage Register (Q3-2026), StatCan table 98-10-0233-01 (period of construction by census subdivision), Brampton and Mississauga second-unit registries, Toronto Fire incident data.
- **Platform coverage.** A separate agent pulled the Canada LSA list from Google's own help pages, Houzz's pro-directory sitemaps (62 categories, ~250 project-type facets) and Yelp's category tree with country availability.
- **Limits.** The session's shared web-search allowance (200 calls) ran out partway through every cluster; later checks used direct fetches of official and known pages. HomeStars, Reddit, CanLII and e-Laws blocked fetches. Web search runs from a US vantage and shows organic links only.

## 5. The web: how the niches link, and how it grows without becoming doorway pages

Four node types: **S**ervice, **U**se, **O**utcome, **C**ity.

- **Money pages are S × C**, and only where a page clears the publish gates (three populated data modules, a sellable provider in the database, dedup linter, schema, link minimums). Nothing here changes that.
- **Hubs are S** (national) plus **cluster hubs** that group services sharing the same specialists.
- **Guides carry U and O nodes.** A use or outcome becomes a guide, city-agnostic by default. It gets its own city pages only when the law or a program differs by municipality: legal basement apartment rules differ in Toronto, Mississauga and Brampton; the Toronto flooding subsidy (C$6,650) has no equivalent in Vaughan and a different one in Mississauga (C$7,500) and Halton (C$675 toward a backwater valve).
- **Edges are the product.** Three edge types recur in every cluster:
  1. *Same specialist does both* — limewash and venetian plaster; underpinning, walkouts, egress windows and interior waterproofing; repointing, lintels and chimney rebuilds; Type 3 abatement firms for asbestos, vermiculite, UFFI, mould and lead.
  2. *This problem needs one of these services* — basement flooding fans out to backwater valve, sump, exterior waterproofing, regrading and a french drain; "cold rooms in an old house" fans out to boiler replacement, ductless heads, high-velocity AC and insulation.
  3. *This project triggers that requirement* — a basement apartment forces ceiling height (underpinning), egress (window well), fire separation (STC 50 and Type X drywall), and a designated substance survey on a pre-1990 house; a heat pump forces a 200 A upgrade; insulating an attic forces vermiculite removal first.

**Why this grows faster than it publishes.** Adding one service node adds its city pages plus edges into every existing outcome and use node it serves; adding one outcome guide adds one URL that links into many existing money pages. Link density rises faster than page count, and internal links from already-indexed pages are what get new pages crawled and indexed fast. The dedup linter and the §3.3 batch gate keep this from turning into a doorway network: a use or outcome stays a section or FAQ until Search Console shows it earning its own impressions.

**The hubs this pass found** (full trees in the evidence files):

- **Basement headroom** (c1 hub A, c6 "add a unit"): low basement ceiling → underpinning / bench footing / house lifting → separate entrance → egress windows → basement apartment legalization → fire separation → multiplex, garden and laneway suites. Shared data: OBC 1.95 m (1.85 m under beams), Fire Code 9.8 date test, municipal second-unit matrix, permit counts, development-charge exemptions. Shared specialists: underpinning contractors (who also sell walkouts, egress and waterproofing), permit designers, sprinkler contractors.
- **Basement water** (c1 hubs B–D): stop basement flooding → backwater valve + sump (subsidy by city) → weeping tile → exterior or interior waterproofing (EXACT category, dense supply: enter through the outcome and subsidy pages) → french drain, yard drainage, regrading, permeable driveway, rain garden. Shared specialists: City-licensed drain contractors (Toronto licences T87, T92, T94), waterproofers.
- **Old-house hazards and insurance** (c4 hub A): insurer or inspector flagged it → knob-and-tube, aluminum wiring, vermiculite/asbestos, UFFI, lead, oil tank, Kitec/Poly-B/galvanized, mould. Shared specialists: ESA electricians, Type 3 abatement firms, TSSA fuel contractors, plumbers. Order-of-work rules are content: remove vermiculite and knob-and-tube before insulating; upgrade to 200 A before a heat pump.
- **Century-home comfort and electrification** (c4 hub B): cold rooms / no ductwork → high-velocity AC, ductless, boiler replacement, heat pump (oil-heated homes get the C$7,500 rebate; gas homes C$2,000), geothermal → spray foam, flat-roof insulation, HRV → deep energy retrofit (HELP loans to C$125k). Rosters: HRSP participating contractors.
- **Century-home exterior** (c2): "do I need a heritage permit" (the regulatory spine) → repointing ← crumbling brick, white stains → spalled brick, stone, lintels ← cracked lintel; leaning chimney → rebuild vs remove; drafty windows in a heritage district → restoration → storm windows; rotting porch → porch restoration → front steps; slate ↔ copper and standing seam ↔ built-in gutters; leaking flat roof → flat roof replacement; ice dams → prevention retrofit. Data: Heritage Register (12,332 address points, 92% in the former City of Toronto), 27–29 Heritage Conservation Districts, Heritage Grant (50% to C$20k, 2026 deadline Nov 7), the 2027-01-01 delisting deadline for ~4,000 listed properties.
- **Plaster and mineral finishes** (c3): limewash + venetian plaster (launch-10) → lime plaster, tadelakt, microcement; roman clay and marmorino as sections; cracked plaster in a century home → plasterers; popcorn ceiling → asbestos check → removal or stretch ceiling. Shared specialists are few and named in the evidence file.
- **Sound** (c3): noisy condo neighbour, echoey room → soundproofing → home theatre, home office, drum room, basement-suite STC 50. Data: OBC STC 50, condo underlay minimums (IIC 50–60), Condominium Authority Tribunal jurisdiction, Toronto noise bylaw Ch. 591.
- **Driveways and front yards** (c5): no parking on my street → parking pad (permit + permeable rule + neighbour poll) → herringbone/clay pavers (launch-10) → permeable pavers, heated driveway, interlock relevelling ← sunken interlock, tree roots lifting driveway → tree permits (bylaw changed 2026-09-01).
- **Trees, ravines, water features, backyard living** (c5): arborist reports and tree permits; ravine restoration (Ravine bylaw); pool nobody uses → pool removal → garden suite or sport court; natural pools ↔ ponds (60 cm pool-fencing rule; Toronto requires fencing, Barrie does not); outdoor kitchens, gas-only outdoor fireplaces in Toronto, louvered pergolas, rooftop decks, garden studios.
- **Add space, stay put, after a fire** (c6): house too small → second-storey, rear and heritage additions, dormers; downsizing in place / parents moving in → aging-in-place, stair lifts, elevators, in-law and garden suites (Home Accessibility Tax Credit is 14% for 2026, C$2,800 maximum, not the C$1,500–3,000 most GTA pages quote); insurance rebuild after fire → rebuild contractor, public adjuster, demolition (82–105 Toronto house fires a year with losses ≥ C$100k).

## 6. GTA-first: recommended pass-1 priority

**Two readings of "GTA first", and which one this report uses.** Addendum A7 rejected "Toronto × all services" in favour of 10 niches × 30–50 cities, because rare-niche demand per metro is thin and authority accrues per topic. This report keeps that geometry and reads "GTA first" as the *priority and data lens*: the 13 GTA municipalities already in the city list become the first pages built for every niche, GTA-specific modules are built first, and Toronto stays the first lead-sales territory. Changing the publish geometry instead would amend §3.3 and A7 and needs the owner's written sign-off. Note that for the suites hub "GTA" genuinely means the 905: Brampton and Mississauga hold the second-unit volume, not Toronto.

**A. Launch-10: unchanged, with GTA-specific corrections.**
- None of the ten is an EXACT category; radon is NONE. The lens confirms the P0 selection.
- Radon: Toronto averages 43 Bq/m³ and about 1 in 22 homes exceeds the guideline (a contractor blog's 19% is wrong). GTA radon pages must say so in the honesty block; lead economics in the GTA are weak, and the new-build radon rough-in (2024 OBC) is the timely angle.
- Slate and heritage windows: pages clear the data bar only in Toronto, Hamilton, Guelph, Oshawa and Kitchener. The Heritage Grant (slate named, 50% to C$20k, deadline Nov 7, 2026) is the strongest module.
